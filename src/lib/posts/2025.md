This post is almost 3 months late, which I’m not happy about, but it is a good reflection of my priorities as of late. Had I written this last year it would have almost certainly come out on time, and probably would have more goals checked off, but the person writing it would have completely bored, burned out, and wishing for the exact life I have right now.

2025 was my first year of true autonomy. No set schedule, no one tracking progress, no one pushing me to do more. This is an inflection point everyone reaches and, in my eyes, is a point in which you’re asked to set the tone for your life. This year, I was lucky enough to experience two completely different worlds.

Comfortmaxxing (Feb-July ‘25): Worked 20 hours a week, woke up at noon, 3 hours a day in the gym, no set schedule, no responsibility, trying any random hobby.

Careermaxxing (July-Feb ‘26) - Worked 60 hours a week, woke up at 8, bed by 11, gym 4x week, hobbies on a premium.

While the latter half of the year was 100x more fulfilling, the first 6 months were by no means a “failure”. In fact, it’s what a ton of people strive for. 75k fully remote out of college, on my way to a promotion within a year, body in great condition, learning guitar in my free time. But it’s just not what I wanted. I had given my all to tech over the last 3 years and stopping at a modest job felt like a waste of a passion. I moved for work in July. I tripled my hours, tripled my rent, killed my job security in a horrible market, and did it all for a 30% raise. In most people’s eyes, this is a bad life decision, and in no way did it make financial sense, but on a 10 year scale it was a pretty clear decision to me.

You see, last summer I got a taste of what it was like to live in a city among some of the most ambitious people in the world, and as corny and delusional as they are, they’re sometimes right. I moved to the city because the floor here is above the ceiling back home, and I knew that if I stayed, the details of what happened in a year mattered less than the osmosis of the city over a decade.

With that said, I got pretty lucky on my first spin. In just one year, I got to experience more stages of a career than a lot will in their whole life.

1. Full remote, 20 hour weeks (amazing team, just not for me)
2. Move to a new city full time
3. Seed startup, 6 people, 60 hours a week, 6 days a week
4. Founding team, take over customer success, deployments, and new features in a matter of months. Tons of autonomy, worked harder and faster than ever.
5. Company gets acquired by a series C startup, higher stakes, harder problems
6. Team grows 100%, leading a small team myself, huge growth opportunities

Considering what happened in a single year, it’s hard for me to fathom that careers are 30+ years long. I have no idea how I’m supposed to keep this pace, so stay tuned for next years update.

## Last years goals

The goals I set in 2024 for this year were apparently mutually exclusive. During my interviews for Grove, I spoke to a recruiter from one of their investors who I told all of these goals to and he basically told me I’d have to give up on all of them, and he was unfortunately right. Achieving 2 of my goals instantly removed my ability to accomplish any of the others. 

1. Move out of Michigan ✅
2. Involve myself in a startup ✅
3. Everything else (learn a language, dunk, learn guitar, learn hardware, get a new artistic hobby) ❌

## Goals for 2026

As arbitrary as my goals have become, and as little value I place on completing them, here are some things I think would make me cooler if I completed in 2026.

1. Climb a v7 boulder
2. Visit China
3. Feel confident in pickup basketball (get a slight bag)
4. Dunk and or do a handstand pushup (still a pipe dream)
5. Talk to more strangers/make a solid friend group
6. Become half decent at surfing
7. 20 minute 5k (I hope this doesn’t happen)
8. Talk more with people building cool things

I don’t expect every year to be like this one, but I’m living in the most delusional city in the US so why not hope.
